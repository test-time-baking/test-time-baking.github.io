## TL;DR

**Test-time Baking (TTB)** turns cross-view attention layers into fixed-size MLP layers at test time, each fit like a light field in latent space. Rendering then needs no 3D representation and costs the same at any number of input views, at the quality of the KV-cache model it starts from.

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Decoder-only Large View Synthesis Models (LVSMs) with KV-cache have recently achieved state-of-the-art quality by regressing novel views from neural networks without reconstructing 3D geometry, while Test-Time Training (TTT) layers save computation but sacrifice quality. We propose Test-Time Baking (TTB), a strategy that learns to bake global scene information from cross-view attention into a lightweight MLP, enabling fast and high-fidelity novel-view decoding. The first key is to repurpose the fast-weight MLP of TTT to learn the cross-view attention mapping explicitly from its input-output pairs, rather than replacing attention entirely, achieving the same rendering efficiency as TTT while delivering substantially higher quality. The second key addresses a modality misalignment between context prefilling and novel-view rendering: we disentangle context tokens into camera-only context rendering tokens to serve as TTB input, and context ground-truth tokens with image content to serve as the TTB mapping target, ensuring the baked MLPs are relieved of the modality generalization challenge between context and novel-view processing. Our method achieves up to 23× rendering speedup with quality on par with the state of the art across several benchmarks.

</details>

## Motivation

Large view synthesis models, which render novel views without an explicit 3D representation, have caught up with per-scene optimization. Given 64 photos of a DL3DV scene and one minute, a KV-cache model ([DVSM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29891)) reaches 31.1 dB. 3D Gaussian Splatting reaches 27.3 dB from the same 64 photos, and 30.5 dB when optimized on about 300.

### Why the KV-cache slows down

A large view synthesis model **reconstructs** a scene once, then **renders** many novel views from that state. The state is not a 3D model: a KV-cache model stores the scene as the keys and values of every input token, and every frame attends to all of it, so each added photo makes every frame slower. TTB instead bakes the scene into **fast weights**: one small MLP per block, written at test time. Their size is fixed, so more photos change what the weights hold, not how many there are.

## Method

### Baking attention into fast weights

TTB builds on Test-Time Training (TTT) layers, which replace attention with a small MLP and train it on the input views at test time. TTT asks the MLP to map each token's key to its own value. TTB keeps cross-view attention running during reconstruction and asks the MLP to reproduce the **attention output** instead. Once the MLP is trained the KV-cache is dropped, so rendering costs exactly what TTT costs, while the weights hold what attention gathered from all views.

<Stage>
<div class="grid gap-4">
  <EqCard kicker="TTT (LaCT)" note="The target is a linear projection of the input tokens.">

$$
\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\,\nabla_{\theta}\,\mathcal{L}\big(f_{\theta}(W_k X),\; W_v X\big)
$$

  </EqCard>
  <EqCard kicker="TTB (ours)" ours note="The target is the cross-view attention output, which already aggregates all context views.">

$$
\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\,\nabla_{\theta}\,\mathcal{L}\big(f_{\theta}(W_k X),\; \textcolor{#4f7d00}{\mathrm{CrossAttn}(X^{*})}\big)
$$

  </EqCard>
</div>
</Stage>

### Context pairing

One mismatch remains. During reconstruction the MLP sees tokens that carry **image and pose**; at render time it is queried with **pose only**. TTB therefore gives every context view two tokens: a pose-only *rendering* token that goes into the MLP, and a *ground-truth* token with the image that feeds the attention target. The MLP is trained on the same kind of input it is later asked about.

The plots under each variant compare reconstruction and rendering tokens at blocks 1, 4 and 7. Only full TTB mixes the two from the first block; the other two keep them apart until late in the network.

$$
X^{(\text{ctx-gt})}_0 = \mathrm{LN}\big(\mathrm{PE}^{(\text{ray})}(P, K) + \mathrm{PE}^{(\text{rgb})}(I)\big), \qquad
X^{(\text{ctx-rend})}_0 = \mathrm{LN}\big(\mathrm{PE}^{(\text{ray})}(P, K)\big)
$$

Each TTB layer bakes the map $X^{(\text{ctx-rend})} \mapsto \mathrm{CrossAttn}(X^{(\text{ctx-gt})})$ and shares the baked MLP with the novel-view tokens, so both stages see the same modality. Reconstruction handles twice the tokens; rendering cost is unchanged.

## Benchmarks

We evaluate on the 140 held-out DL3DV scenes at 960p with 16, 32 and 64 input views. **TTB renders every setting at 23 fps, up to 23× faster than the KV-cache model it starts from ([DVSM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29891) in the table) and 2.3× faster than [LaCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23884), within 1 dB of the KV-cache's quality.** With local-duplicate inference, which keeps one copy of the fast weights per 16 views, it passes the KV-cache at 64 views (31.33 vs 31.11 dB). The cost is paid once, at reconstruction: 65 s for 64 views, against 60 s for the KV-cache and 7.3 s for LaCT.

## Ablation

Three additive changes turn a TTT replacement into TTB: context pairing, an MSE inner loop with three steps, and the cross-view attention target. Together they close the gap to the KV-cache model at TTT's rendering speed.

| :--- | :---: | :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| TTT | 1 | dot-prod | 23.12 | 0.704 | 0.286 |
| TTT | 3 | MSE | 23.39 | 0.715 | 0.277 |
| TTB-- | 1 | dot-prod | 23.78 | 0.728 | 0.264 |
| TTB-- | 2 | MSE | 24.16 | 0.744 | 0.251 |

| TTB-- | 5 | MSE | **24.29** | **0.751** | **0.246** |
